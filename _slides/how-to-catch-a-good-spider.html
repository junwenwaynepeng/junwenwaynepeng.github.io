--- layout: slide title: | Catching a spider: Automating the Spider Algorithm in Complex Dynamics date: 2025-8-26s author: Wayne Peng coauthors: to: National University of Kaohsiung description: Some slides for a git and GitHub workshop using reveal.js categories: academic abstract: | The Spider Algorithm is a well-known topological tool used to study the dynamics of polynomials and rational functions. However, implementing this algorithm computationally requires a system capable of distinguishing valid homotopic paths from tangled, intersecting ones. In this talk, we explore a computational framework that translates the continuous topological constraints of complex dynamics into discrete graph-theoretic algorithms. We will outline the process of generating discretized paths (spider legs) from a chosen base point to the post-critical set of a rational map, utilizing projective coordinates and the chordal metric to seamlessly handle limits at infinity. We then introduce an algorithmic method to evaluate the resulting monodromy graphs. By searching for vertex-disjoint paths and applying cyclic ordering, our algorithm programmatically verifies the existence of a "good spider" without human visual intervention. This provides a robust computational pipeline for exploring the Iterated Monodromy Groups of high-degree rational functions. tags: - research - academic - slides front-img: "/assets/img/slides/how-to-catch-a-good-spider/automata-1.png" ---

Three steps to define IMG action

STEP 1

Give a collection of paths $\mathcal{L}=\{l_x\}_{x\in f^{-1}(t)} where $l_x$ starts at $t$ and ends at $x$.

STEP 2

Consider a spider and attach the foot of every leg with a small circle around each pole. These are loops starting from $t$ and then walk along legs and circle around a pole and travel back along the legs. Clearly, these loops are generators $g$ of $\pi_1(\mathcal{M},t)$

STEP 3

The preimages of $g$ under $f$ and the paths $l_x$ forms a new loop start from $t$. Suppose one of the preimages walks from $x$ to $y$ for some $x,y\in f^{-1}(t)$. Denote this preimage by $f^{-1}(g)[x]$ and $f^{-1}(g)[x]\cdot x=y$. Then, $l_xf^{-1}(g)[x]l_y^{-1}$ becomes a loop, denoted by $g'$. Then, we get the following relationship: considering a word $xw$, we have $$(xw)^g = yw^{g'}.$$
