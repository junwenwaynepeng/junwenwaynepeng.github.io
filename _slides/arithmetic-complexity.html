--- layout: slide title: | Explicit Arithmetic Distance Motivated by Tits Alternative in Arithmetic Dynamics date: 2026-5-12 author: Wayne Peng coauthors: to: National Central University description: Some slides for a git and GitHub workshop using reveal.js categories: academic abstract: | Motivating by the study of Tits alternative in arithmetic dynamics, we investigate the arithmetic distance between distinct rational maps $f,\ g: \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})\to \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$ defined by $$\delta(f,g)=\sup_{x\in \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})}|\hat{h}_f(x)-\hat{h}_g(x)|$$ Moreover, when $g$ is monomial, the arithmetic distance is invariant under conjugation by elements of $\operatorname{PGL}_2$. This motivate the definition of the arithmetic complexity of a conjugacy class $[f]$ in the moduli space $\mathcal{M}_d$ by $$\mu([f]) =\delta(f,g)$$ To compute this arithmetic complexity, we establish explicit and computable Hölder constants and exponents for the modified Green function with respect to the chordal metric. Under additional assumptions, we further obtain Lipschitz continuity. Using these analytic estimates, we develop a numerical algorithm to compute arithmetic distances between rational maps with good reduction at all finite places. The method combines a global coarse search with local optimization on $\mathbf{P}^1(\mathbb{Q})$. We then apply the algorithm to families of unicritical maps $f:x\mapsto x^d+c$ and find $\mu([f])$. Our Result

(Bell-Huang-P.-Tom)
Let $f$ and $g$ be rational functions of degrees greater than $2$. Let $\hat{h_f}$ and $\hat{h_g}$ be the canonical height of $f$ and $g$ respectively. We have $\hat{h_f}\neq \hat{h_g}$ if and only if there exists an integer $j$ such that $\langle f^j,g^j\rangle$ is a rank 2 free generated semigroup.

Let $\omega=\phi_m\cdots\phi_1$ where $\phi_j$ is equal to $f$ or $g$ for each $i$. Let $s_j=\phi_j\cdots \phi_1$ for $j\leq m$.

Let $h$ be the Weil height. Let $C_f$ be the constant such that $$|h(f(a))-dh(a)|< C_f.$$

Then, we have $$ \left|\dfrac{h(w(\alpha))}{\deg\omega}-\dfrac{h(s_j(\alpha))}{\deg s_j}\right|<\dfrac{\max\{C_f,C_g\}}{\min\{\deg(f),\deg(g)\}^j} $$ for $j=1,\ldots,m$.

We choose an $\varepsilon< |h_f(\alpha)-h_g(\alpha)|/2\neq 0$. Then $B(h_f,\varepsilon)\cap B(h_g,\varepsilon)=\emptyset$.

By the inequality in the previous slides, we know there is a $j$ such that, for all words starting with $j$ many $f$, their canonical heights at $\alpha$ will stay in $B(h_f,\varepsilon)$.

Similarly, for all words starting with $j$ many $g$, their canonical heights at $\alpha$ will stay in $B(h_g,\varepsilon)$.

Thus we can conclude that any word starting with $j$ many $f$ won't equal to one starting with $j$ many $g$.

Fix a constant $B>0$ and an integer $d>1$. For any number field $K$, we have $C_f>B$ for all but finitely many degree $d>1$ rational maps $f:\mathbb{P}^1(K)\to\mathbb{P}^1(K)$.
If one of the degrees of $f$ or $g$ is strickly greather than $3$, then the semigroup $\langle f,g\rangle$ is free for all but finitely many $f$ and $g$. In particular, the semigroup $\langle f^2, g\rangle$ is free for all but finitely many $f$.

We have $$ \dfrac{C_f}{d}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{d-1}\right)<|h_f(a)-h(a)|<\dfrac{C_f}{d-1} $$ and $$ \left|h_f(P)-\dfrac{h(f^i(P))}{d^i}\right|<\dfrac{C_f}{d^i(d-1)} $$ for $a\in\mathbb{P}^1(\bar{Q})$. It follows that $$ \begin{align*} |\hat{h}_f(P)-\hat{h}_g(P)|&\geq |\hat{h}_f(P)-h(P)|-|h(P)-\hat{h}_g(P)|\\ &\geq \dfrac{C_f}{d_f}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{d_f-1}\right)-\frac{C_g}{d_g-1}. \end{align*} $$ By the lemma, we have $$ C_f>\frac{d_f(d_f-1)(d_g+1)}{(d_g-3)d_g(d_g-1)}C_g $$ for all but finitely many $g$ with $d_g>3$.

Putting these inequality together, we have $$ \begin{align*} \dfrac{C_f}{d_f}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{d_f-1}\right)-&\frac{C_g}{d_g-1} = \frac{(d_f-2)d_g(d_g-1)C_f-d_f(d_f-1)d_g C_g}{d_f(d_f-1)d_g(d_g-1)}\\ =& \frac{d_g(d_g-1)C_fi+d_fi(d_f-1)C_g+d_g(d_g-1)(d_f-3)C_f-d_f(d_f-1)(d_g+1)C_g}{d_f(d_f-1)d_g(d_g-1)}\\ >& \frac{C_f}{d_f(d_f-1)}+\frac{C_g}{d_g(d_g-1)}. \end{align*} $$
